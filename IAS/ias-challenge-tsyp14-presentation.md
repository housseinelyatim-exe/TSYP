# IAS Challenge — TSYP14 Technical Challenge

## Overview

The **IAS Challenge** is the TSYP14 Technical Challenge, organised under IEEE Region 8, IEEE Tunisia Section, IEEE Young Professionals Tunisia Section, INSAT IEEE Student Branch, and the **IAS Tunisia Section Chapter (IEEE Industry Applications Society)**.

**Scope & topic:** *Engineering Industry for a Dynamic Future.* Industrial systems are designed to operate efficiently under defined conditions, but real-world industrial environments continuously evolve due to changing demands, unexpected disruptions, resource constraints, operational challenges, and technological advances. The challenge asks teams to develop innovative engineering solutions addressing real industrial needs, with emphasis on:

- **Resilience**
- **Efficiency**
- **Sustainability**
- **Digitalisation**

**Challenge problem:** Teams start from an industrial problem or process — preferably from a real industrial environment, or from an organiser-provided / organiser-validated industrial case. The solution must follow a research- and process-based methodology:

> Industrial Problem → Process Mapping → Research & State of the Art → Solution Exploration → Comparative Analysis → Solution Development → Validation → Impact Assessment

**Challenge goal:** Develop innovative, practical, research-driven and technically sound solutions that address real industrial challenges and prepare industrial systems for a more dynamic and demanding future.

**Three Focus Areas** (all follow the same core approach: Industrial Problem → Process Mapping → Research & State of the Art → Solution Development → Demonstration → Impact Assessment):

1. Resilient Industrial Systems
2. Efficient & Sustainable Industrial Systems
3. Digitalised Industrial Systems

## Challenge Methodology (8 steps)

Every solution — regardless of focus area — must be developed through these eight steps:

| # | Step | Description |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | Industrial Problem | Identify and clearly characterize a relevant industrial problem |
| 2 | Process Mapping | Map and analyse the existing **AS-IS** process, including information flows, resources, constraints and operational bottlenecks |
| 3 | Research & State of the Art | Investigate relevant scientific research, standards, patents, modern industrial practices and existing solutions |
| 4 | Solution Exploration | Explore possible engineering solutions |
| 5 | Comparative Analysis | Compare the proposed alternatives and justify the selected approach |
| 6 | Solution Development | Develop the engineering solution and demonstrate its feasibility |
| 7 | Validation | Evaluate the technical solution and its performance |
| 8 | Impact Assessment | Assess quantitative impact, including relevant technical, economic and environmental metrics |

## Challenge Phases — Breakdown

### Phase 1: Selection — Technical Poster (25 pts)

Teams submit a **technical poster** focused on:
- Relevance of the industrial problem
- AS-IS process mapping
- Literature and state-of-the-art research
- Validation of the proposed methodology

**Deliverable:** Technical Poster

### Phase 2: Development — Technical Report & Proof of Concept (45 pts)

Teams develop their solution through:
- A **5–7 page** expanded technical report
- Implementation of the **Proof of Concept (PoC)**
- Technical prototype / simulation / demonstration
- Technical presentation

The report and PoC must demonstrate the progression from the initial **AS-IS** process toward the proposed **TO-BE** process.

**Deliverable:** 5–7 page Technical Report + Proof of Concept

### Phase 3: Final — MVP & IAS Session at TSYP14 (30 pts)

Teams must demonstrate:
- A mature and functional **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**
- Quantitative impact assessment
- Technical, economic and environmental evaluation
- Final presentation and pitch

Finalists must additionally provide:
- Pitching & Q&A
- Complete physical prototype
- Proposed solution write-up
- Technologies used
- Project architecture
- User manual
- GitHub Repository

**Deliverable:** Minimum Viable Product (MVP) + Final Pitch

**Pitching format:** English · **5 min pitching** · **5 min Q&A**

### Supporting materials (any phase)

Presentation · GitHub Repository · Hardware or software prototype · Report · Demo · PoC · Pitching video

> Deliverables follow a progressive approach according to each phase's requirements.

## Tools, Knowledge & Hardware Needed

Based on the deliverables and methodology required at each phase, teams should plan for:

**Knowledge / methodology skills**
- Industrial process analysis (AS-IS / TO-BE mapping): information flows, resource constraints, bottleneck identification
- Literature and state-of-the-art research skills: scientific papers, standards, patents, benchmarking against modern industrial practice
- Comparative analysis / decision methodology to justify a chosen engineering solution among 2–3 alternatives
- Engineering domain knowledge relevant to the chosen focus area (resilience, efficiency/sustainability, or digitalisation of industrial systems)
- Technical/economic/environmental impact assessment (quantitative metrics)
- English technical writing (5–7 page report) and English pitching/public speaking (5 min pitch + 5 min Q&A)

**Tools**
- Documentation & poster design tools for the Phase 1 technical poster
- Report-writing / technical documentation tooling for the Phase 2 report
- **GitHub** for version-controlled code repository (explicitly required as a deliverable/supporting material)
- Simulation, prototyping, or modeling software appropriate to the chosen industrial problem (e.g., process simulation, CAD, control/automation software, data/digitalisation tooling) depending on focus area
- Presentation software for the technical presentation and final pitch; video tools if submitting a pitching video

**Hardware**
- Access to (or data from) a real industrial process/environment, or use of the organiser-provided/validated industrial case
- Hardware or software prototype components needed to build the Proof of Concept (Phase 2) and the physical/functional MVP (Phase 3) — the exact hardware depends on the team's chosen problem and focus area (e.g., sensors, controllers, embedded/IoT hardware for digitalisation projects; mechanical/process equipment for resilience or efficiency projects)

## Why IEEE ENSI SB Should Engage

- **Direct fit with IAS's mission:** this is the flagship technical challenge of the IAS Tunisia Section Chapter at TSYP14 — a high-visibility opportunity to represent ENSI SB within the IEEE Region 8 / IEEE Tunisia ecosystem.
- **Structured, mentorable format:** the 3-phase, 8-step methodology (Poster → Report/PoC → MVP/Pitch) gives our members a clear, coachable engineering process rather than an open-ended competition — good for building a team pipeline over time.
- **Real prize and recognition:** 300 TND for 1st place plus certificates for top-3, with the final pitch happening live at TSYP14 in front of the IEEE Tunisia community.
- **Small team size (max 4)** keeps commitment manageable — we could realistically field one or more teams from different focus areas (Resilience / Efficiency & Sustainability / Digitalisation).
- **Skill-building alignment:** the challenge exercises exactly the skills ENSI SB should be cultivating — industrial process analysis, applied research, PoC/MVP engineering, GitHub-based collaboration, and English technical pitching.
