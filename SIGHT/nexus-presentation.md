# NEXUS: Next-Generation Emergency eXchange for Universal Sustainability — TSYP14 Technical Challenge
### Presentation for the IEEE ENSI Student Branch Board

---

## Overview

**NEXUS** is a TSYP14 technical challenge organized by **IEEE SIGHT (Special Interest Group on Humanitarian Technology) Tunisia Section Chapter**, in collaboration with **IEEE Tech4Good**, **IEEE CEDA (Council on Electronic Design Automation)**, and the broader TSYP14 ecosystem (IEEE Region 8, IEEE Tunisia Section, IEEE Young Professionals, INSAT IEEE Student Branch).

**Scope & Topic:** Tunisia faces increasing climate and infrastructure risks — **flash floods, wildfires, drought and dam shortfalls, coastal erosion, summer heatwaves**. The challenge focuses on building **low-cost, low-power smart systems** that connect sensing, communication, and response into one resilient system. Teams apply systems thinking to detect a crisis early and deliver actionable information to the people who can respond.

**Choose one application area:**
- **Environmental & Climate Sensing** — flood, wildfire, drought, or air/water-quality monitoring
- **Disaster Response Coordination** — search-and-rescue, logistics, and first-responder coordination
- **Critical Infrastructure Resilience** — water networks, dams, power grids, or buildings
- **Public Health & Emergency Monitoring** — heatwave, epidemic, or air-quality alerts
- **Community-Deployable Resilience Systems** — solar-powered, offline-capable, locally maintainable systems

Solutions are **strongly encouraged** to address a **real Tunisian region, hazard, or community**, and at least **one UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG)**.

**Challenge problem:** Disaster losses are often not caused by a lack of data, but by data that does not reach an early-warning system, responder, or household in time to act. Teams must design a smart solution that:

`Detects → Communicates → Coordinates → Enables Response`

The solution should be designed for **real conditions**: intermittent power, limited connectivity, local maintenance capacity, and affordability.

---

## Challenge Goals

1. **Sense Before It Escalates** — detect hazards (flood, fire, drought, structural stress, heat, pollution) early enough for meaningful action.
2. **Design for Tunisia's Reality** — build for intermittent power, intermittent connectivity, local maintenance, affordability, and real field conditions.
3. **Connect Sensing to Action** — ensure information reaches the appropriate household, municipality, civil protection, or farmer in a usable form and in time to act.
4. **Engineer the Whole System** — apply systems architecture, systems thinking, risk management, and lifecycle resilience.
5. **Advance the SDGs** — explicitly connect the solution to relevant UN Sustainable Development Goals and demonstrate measurable community benefit.

## Tools, Knowledge & Hardware Needed

**Domain knowledge:**
- Climate/disaster-risk context for Tunisia (flash floods, wildfires, drought, coastal erosion, heatwaves) and ideally a specific real region/community/hazard to target.
- UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) — to explicitly map the solution to at least one, ideally more for bonus points.
- Basic business/entrepreneurship skills: market analysis, financial/ROI modeling, risk mitigation planning, partner identification, Mission/Vision/Values framing.
- Humanitarian and ethical design principles: equity, transparency, community ownership, inclusion, local empowerment.

**Technical / engineering skills:**
- Low-power embedded systems design (sensor nodes designed for intermittent power and offline operation).
- Sensing relevant to the chosen application area (e.g., water level, temperature/humidity, air/water quality, structural strain, seismic/vibration).
- Low-power communication protocols (e.g., LoRa/LoRaWAN or similar) suited for intermittent connectivity and rural/remote deployment.
- Optionally, edge AI/processing for early hazard detection.
- Systems architecture and lifecycle-resilience thinking — designing for local maintainability and affordability, not just a lab demo.
- Circuit/system diagramming and technical documentation for the GitHub repository.

**Hardware:**
- A real, working sensing and/or communication component is mandatory (pure software/app submissions are rejected).
- Solar power or another low-power/energy-harvesting setup is strongly aligned with "Community-Deployable Resilience Systems" and the "design for intermittent power" goal.
- Low-cost, field-deployable hardware suited to local maintenance and affordability constraints (the challenge explicitly penalizes solutions that aren't realistic for Tunisian field conditions).
