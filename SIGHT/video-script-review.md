# Video script review (2-minute limit)

## Verdict
Good structure and a strong ending, but there are four problems:
1. **Too long.** 320 words is about **2:17** of speech at a normal pace (140 words/min), before any music or pauses.
2. **One fact can't be backed up.** "Vehicles were swept away or crushed" isn't in any of our verified sources.
3. **It tells the opposite story to the report.** The script says the problem is *detecting* danger. Our strongest point is that **a warning existed the day before 2018 and people still died**. The failure was the last mile.
4. **Unfinished parts.** The researcher has no name (there's an empty gap: "this field  ."), the timestamps overlap (1:00 starts before 1:05 ends), and there's a gap at 1:35–1:40.

## Revised script (228 words, about 1:40 of speech, leaving ~20 s for music and visuals)

**[0:00–0:12 | Hook]**
Droughts. Wildfires. Coastal erosion. Flash floods.
Of all climate hazards, flash floods leave the least time to react.

**[0:12–0:42 | Nabeul, 2018]**
On the 22nd of September 2018, about 200 millimetres of rain fell on Nabeul in a single day. Six people died.
Yet a warning had been issued the day before.
The forecast was not what failed. What failed was the last step: getting a clear instruction to the right people, and making sure someone acted on it.

**[0:42–0:55 | Research]**
We studied existing early-warning systems, and interviewed **[Name]**, a researcher in **[field]**, to adapt our solution to local conditions.

**[0:55–1:40 | Our solution]**
So we built more than a sensor: a complete warning chain.
Three solar stations watch the wadi. Upstream, one sees the flood coming. Midstream, one confirms it. At the road bridge, a beacon and siren warn drivers instantly, even with no internet and no mobile network.
No alarm on a single reading: two independent signals must agree.
Volunteers and officials must confirm every alert. If nobody answers within three minutes, it escalates automatically, to the municipality, then Civil Protection. Silence is never treated as safe.
And the authorities keep the final decision on public alerts.

**[1:40–2:00 | Closing]**
Our goal is not to measure water. It is to give people time to act.
Behind every warning is a family.
We cannot stop every flood. But we can stop a natural hazard from becoming a human tragedy.
