# Women And Rural Agriculture Innovation Challenge — TSYP14 Technical Challenge

## Overview

The **Women And Rural Agriculture Innovation Challenge** is a TSYP14 Technical Challenge organised jointly by IEEE Region 8, IEEE Tunisia Section, IEEE Young Professionals Tunisia Section, INSAT IEEE Student Branch, **IEEE WIE (Women in Engineering) Tunisia Affinity Group**, **IEEE Entrepreneurship Tunisia Section**, and the **IEEE TEMS (Technology & Engineering Management Society) / SSIT (Society on Social Implications of Technology) Tunisia Section Joint Chapter**.

**Scope & topic:** the challenge invites engineering and entrepreneurship students to design technology-driven and business-driven solutions that strengthen the position of women in Tunisia's rural and peri-urban agricultural and artisanal economy. It focuses on:

- Technological automation to reduce manual labor and drudgery
- Digitalization & supply-chain structuring
- Occupational health & safety
- Direct market access for women-led ventures

Solutions must consider the real working conditions, safety, and economic empowerment of women.

**Challenge problem:** women agricultural workers, artisanal producers and micro-entrepreneurs play a major role in local production — including **olive oil harvesting, essential oil distillation, textile weaving, and agro-food processing** — but face interconnected challenges:

- Low technological automation
- Fragmented and informal supply chains
- Occupational health risks
- Limited direct access to markets
- Reduced traceability and bargaining power
- Dependence on intermediaries

Addressing only one dimension has limited impact — solutions should consider the interconnected nature of these challenges.

**Challenge goal:** teams are expected to develop practical and deployable solutions that:
- Reduce Physical Drudgery — use technology to reduce manual effort and physical strain
- Improve Occupational Safety — address health risks through protective, monitoring or ergonomic solutions
- Digitalize Supply Chains — improve traceability and connections between producers, processors, distributors and buyers
- Improve Market Access — enable women-led micro-enterprises to reach buyers directly through digital platforms, traceability or alternative distribution models
- Promote Gender-Inclusive Engineering — design around the actual needs and working conditions of women
- Build Deployable Solutions — develop prototypes and business concepts with realistic potential for deployment in rural Tunisian communities

## Thematic Tracks (each team selects one primary track)

| Track | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Track 1** — Low Technological Automation | Develop technologies that reduce manual labor, physical effort and repetitive work |
| **Track 2** — Supply Chain Fragmentation & Limited Direct Market Access | Improve traceability, coordination, distribution and direct access to markets |
| **Track 3** — Occupational Health Risks | Develop protective, monitoring, ergonomic or preventive solutions for safer working conditions |

## Challenge Phases — Breakdown

### Phase 1: Preliminary Solution — 40 pts (Deadline: 05 November 2026)

Teams must submit:
- **3-minute pitching video** showing the progress of the challenge and presenting the technical solution
  - Minimum editing preferred
  - **Must remain anonymous** (identity-blind submission for judging)
- **Technical Report — maximum 6 pages**, with required structure:
  1. Title
  2. Abstract (150–250 words)
  3. Keywords (3–6 keywords)
  4. Introduction
  5. Related Work — if applicable
  6. Methodology / Materials and Methods
  7. Results and Discussion
  8. Conclusion and Future Work
  9. Acknowledgments — optional
  10. References

> ⚠️ **Plagiarism checks will be applied, including internet sources and AI-generated content.**

**Deliverables:** 3-minute Pitching Video + Technical Report (max. 6 pages)

### Phase 2: Solution Deployment — 60 pts (Deadline: 10 December 2026)

Pre-selected teams must:
- Develop and **deploy** the technical solution prototype
- Submit a **4-minute presentation** (PDF format)
- Submit a **2-minute final demonstration video**

Teams may also submit additional materials demonstrating impact and creativity (e.g., papers or posters).

Finalists must provide:
- 4-minute Presentation PDF — including proposed solution, technologies used, project architecture and impact
- 2-minute Prototype Demo Video
- Deployed Technical Prototype
- GitHub Repository

**Pitching format:** English · **4 min presentation** · **2 min Q&A**

## Tools, Knowledge & Hardware Needed

**Knowledge / skills**
- Understanding of the socio-economic realities of women in rural/peri-urban agriculture and artisanal work in Tunisia (olive oil, essential oils, textiles, agro-food processing)
- Gender-inclusive / human-centered engineering design methodology
- Business model development and feasibility/impact analysis (entrepreneurship skillset, given the mandatory Entrepreneurship Community membership)
- Technical report writing in the required academic structure (abstract, methodology, results, references) with awareness of plagiarism-detection standards (including AI-content detection)
- Video production and editing (3-min anonymous pitch video in Phase 1; 2-min demo video in Phase 2) — kept minimally edited/authentic per the brief
- English pitching and Q&A presentation skills

**Tools**
- Video recording/editing tools for the two required videos
- Report/document tooling for the 6-page technical report and the 4-minute presentation PDF
- **GitHub** for the Phase 2 code/project repository
- Track-specific software: automation/robotics design tools (Track 1), supply-chain/traceability or e-commerce/digital platform tools (Track 2), or health/safety monitoring & ergonomic design tools (Track 3)

**Hardware** (depends on chosen track)
- Track 1 (automation): mechanical or electromechanical components to reduce manual labor (e.g., motorized/assistive tools for harvesting, distillation, weaving, or processing tasks)
- Track 2 (supply chain/market access): connectivity/traceability hardware if physical tagging or tracking is used (e.g., QR/RFID, IoT sensors), otherwise primarily digital-platform/software-based
- Track 3 (occupational health): wearable or environmental monitoring hardware, protective/ergonomic device prototypes
- A deployed, working prototype is mandatory for Phase 2 — teams should plan hardware sourcing and testing time well before the 10/12/2026 deadline

## Why IEEE ENSI SB Should Engage

- **Cross-affinity-group visibility:** this challenge is co-organised by WIE, Entrepreneurship, TEMS and SSIT — participating lets ENSI SB show engagement across multiple IEEE Tunisia affinity groups and societies at once, not just one.
- **Real social impact angle:** the challenge targets a concrete, underserved population (rural/peri-urban women workers in Tunisian agriculture and artisanal trades) — a strong story for SB outreach, social media, and annual report content beyond the competition itself.
- **Built-in interdisciplinary team-building:** the mandatory mix of WIE + Entrepreneurship + TEMS + SSIT members naturally pushes ENSI SB to form cross-functional teams, strengthening ties between the SB's technical and entrepreneurship/affinity-group members.
- **Manageable team size (3–4)** with two clearly scoped phases makes it approachable for first-time competitors, while the anonymous Phase 1 pitch keeps early judging merit-based.
- **Tangible national platform:** finalists present live at TSYP14 with a deployed prototype — high-visibility recognition for ENSI SB within IEEE Tunisia Section regardless of final placement (prizes still to be defined by organisers).
